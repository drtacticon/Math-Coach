Day 118 · Lesson 13Distinguish Area, Surface Area, and Volume

LearnPracticeDaily PDF ↗
Standards 6.G.A.16.G.A.46.G.A.2

enVision Topic 7: Solve Area, Surface Area, and Volume Problems | Spectrum companion Chapter 6

LEARNING TARGET

I can distinguish Area, Surface Area, and Volume, and explain and check my reasoning.

01 · LEARN

Build the idea

Two matching triangles form a parallelogram, so A = (base × perpendicular height)/2. A slanted side is not necessarily the height. Label area in square units. A closed rectangular prism has two faces of area lw, two of lh, and two of wh. Add the six face areas: SA = 2lw + 2lh + 2wh. If a face is missing, do not include it. A rectangular prism has volume V = lwh = Bh, where B is base area. Fractional edge lengths still multiply. Use cubic units; surface area and volume measure different attributes.

WORKED EXAMPLE 1 · Constructed response

Sketch a triangle with base 8 cm and perpendicular height 5 cm. Find its area.

  1. Two matching triangles form a parallelogram, so A = (base × perpendicular height)/2.
  2. A slanted side is not necessarily the height.
  3. A = bh/2 = 8 × 5 ÷ 2 = 20.
  4. Two such triangles make a parallelogram.
  5. State the result in the form requested: 20 cm².
20 cm²
WORKED EXAMPLE 2 · Numeric entry and model reasoning

A prism measures 11/2 m by 5/2 m by 3/2 m. Part A: enter its volume. Part B: how many cubes with edge 1/2 m fill it? Explain why the cube count differs from the cubic-meter volume.

  1. A rectangular prism has volume V = lwh = Bh, where B is base area.
  2. Multiply edges: 11/2 × 5/2 × 3/2 = 165/8.
  3. The edge counts are 11, 5, 3, giving 165 cubes.
  4. Each cube is 1/8 m³
  5. 165/8 = 165/8.
165/8 m³; 165 cubes
02 · PRACTICE

Your turn

Start with foundations, then apply the idea and explain your reasoning. Select answers below or record them on paper.

  1. Constructed response

    Sketch a triangle with base 8 cm and perpendicular height 7 cm. Find its area.

  2. Constructed response

    A 7 cm by 4 cm base and 4 cm height form a closed box. Draw its net and find the material area. Ignore tabs and overlap.

  3. Constructed response

    A rectangular prism has edges 6, 3, and 4 cm. Sketch it and find its volume.

  4. Constructed response

    Sketch a triangle with base 6 cm and perpendicular height 7 cm. Find its area.

  5. Constructed response

    A 6 cm by 2 cm base and 3 cm height form a closed box. Draw its net and find the material area. Ignore tabs and overlap.

  6. Constructed response

    A rectangular prism has edges 2, 1, and 3 cm. Sketch it and find its volume.

  7. Constructed response

  8. Two-part numeric entry

  9. Numeric entry and model reasoning

    A prism measures 5/2 m by 2 m by 3/2 m. Part A: enter its volume. Part B: how many cubes with edge 1/2 m fill it? Explain why the cube count differs from the cubic-meter volume.

  10. Constructed response

    Sketch a triangle with base 4 cm and perpendicular height 3 cm. Find its area. Explain why the slanted side is not needed.

03 · CHECK

Answers and reasoning

Reveal the teacher answer key
  1. 28 cm²

    A = bh/2 = 8 × 7 ÷ 2 = 28. Two such triangles make a parallelogram.

    6.G.A.1
  2. 144 cm²

    Closed surface area = 2(28+28+16) = 144.

    6.G.A.4
  3. 72 cm³

    B = 6 × 3 = 18 cm²; V = B × 4 = 72 cm³.

    6.G.A.2
  4. 21 cm²

    A = bh/2 = 6 × 7 ÷ 2 = 21. Two such triangles make a parallelogram.

    6.G.A.1
  5. 72 cm²

    Closed surface area = 2(12+18+6) = 72.

    6.G.A.4
  6. 6 cm³

    B = 2 × 1 = 2 cm²; V = B × 3 = 6 cm³.

    6.G.A.2
  7. 35/2 cm²

    A = bh/2 = 10 × 7/2 ÷ 2 = 35/2. Two such triangles make a parallelogram.

    6.G.A.1
  8. 94 cm²; open box 79 cm²

    Closed area = 2(15+20+12) = 94. Subtract exactly one top face, 15, for the open box.

    6.G.A.4
  9. 15/2 m³; 60 cubes

    Multiply edges: 5/2 × 2 × 3/2 = 15/2. The edge counts are 5, 4, 3, giving 60 cubes. Each cube is 1/8 m³; 60/8 = 15/2.

    6.G.A.2
  10. 6 cm²

    A = bh/2 = 4 × 3 ÷ 2 = 6. Two such triangles make a parallelogram.

    6.G.A.1
