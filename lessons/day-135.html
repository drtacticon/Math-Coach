03 · CHECK

Answers and reasoning

Reveal the teacher answer key
  1. 35 m²

    Cut and shift a corner; A = bh = 5 × 7 = 35.

  2. 28 cm³

    B = 2 × 7/2 = 7 cm²; V = B × 4 = 28 cm³.

  3. 2 faces each of 2×3, 2×6, 3×6; 72 cm²

    Add 2(6) + 2(12) + 2(18) = 72.

  4. 56 m²

    Outer area 80 minus cutout 24 = 56.

  5. Yes; plant heights vary.

    Look for a collection of data and anticipated variation; identify the population and variable.

  6. Center near 7/2 min; range 3 min; roughly symmetric; no isolated extreme

    The values balance around the middle. Range = 5 - 2.

  7. n = 6; minutes; mean 19/2 min

    Sum = 57; mean = 57 ÷ 6 = 19/2.

  8. Median 14 min

    6 ordered values: average positions 3 and 4: (12 + 16) ÷ 2 = 14.

  9. Q1 11, Q3 14; IQR 3 min; range 9 min

    Lower half [8, 11, 11] has median 11; upper half [14, 14, 17] has median 14. Subtract Q1 from Q3.

  10. Mean 12 min; MAD 10/3 min

    Distances: 6, 2, 2, 2, 2, 6; sum 20; divide by 6 = 10/3.

  11. Mean 13 min and MAD 5 min; roughly symmetric data

    Mean = 13, median = 13, IQR = 16 - 10 = 6, MAD = 5. Accept another justified choice tied to shape and purpose.

  12. 7: 1 dot; 13: 2 dots; 19: 2 dots; 25: 1 dot; n = 6

    Each observation contributes one dot; stack repeated values at their number-line position.

  13. Frequencies 3, 2, 3, 2

    Count every value once. Bars touch and have equal width; horizontal axis is minutes, vertical axis is frequency.

  14. (6, 7, 15/2, 9, 18); IQR 2 min

    Box runs from 7 to 9, median line at 15/2, whiskers to 6 and 18.

  15. A median 19/2, B median 23/2 min; both IQR 2 min. B is typically 2 min higher; equal middle-half variability.

    Adding 2 shifts all values and the median by 2, but does not change Q3 - Q1.

  16. 6 observations of reading time in minutes. Median 9/2 min, IQR 2 min; right-skewed with a high value 15 min, so median/IQR resist that value.

    Calculate the center and spread before interpreting them in context. Other accurate, justified summaries are acceptable.

  17. Example: On a 1 to 5 scale, how do students in our class rate the school library? Ratings vary among students; the numerical scale must be defined.

    Look for a collection of data and anticipated variation; identify the population and variable.

  18. Center near 9/2 min; range 12 min; right-skewed with high value 15 min

    The values cluster low with a long high tail. Range = 15 - 3.

  19. n = 6; minutes; mean 31/6 min; new mean 37/7 min

    Sum = 31; mean = 31 ÷ 6 = 31/6. New sum = 37; divide by 7.

  20. Median 7/2 min; mean 31/6 min; the high value pulls the mean upward

    6 ordered values: average positions 3 and 4: (3 + 4) ÷ 2 = 7/2.
