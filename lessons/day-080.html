Day 080 · Lesson 05Explain Equivalent Ratios in Two Models

LearnPracticeDaily PDF ↗
Standards 6.RP.A.3a

enVision Topic 5: Understand and Use Ratios and Rates | Spectrum companion Chapter 3

LEARNING TARGET

I can explain Equivalent Ratios in Two Models, and explain and check my reasoning.

01 · LEARN

Build the idea

Multiply both values in a ratio by the same scale factor. Organize pairs in a table and check that every row compares the same quantities in the same order. For a ratio a:b, draw a sections for one quantity and b sections for the other. A total covers a+b sections. Divide the total by a+b to find one section, then multiply by each part count.

WORKED EXAMPLE 1 · Constructed response

A mix uses 6 cups of oats for 6 cups of seeds. Complete the table (oats, seeds): (6, 6), (12, ___), (18, ___).

Step-by-step solution

  1. Multiply both values in a ratio by the same scale factor.
  2. Organize pairs in a table and check that every row compares the same quantities in the same order.
  3. Multiply both parts by 2 and by 3.
Answer: 12; 18
WORKED EXAMPLE 2 · Model and error analysis

The ratio of paper flags to fabric flags is 3:3. There are 12 flags. Part A: draw a tape diagram and enter each count. Part B: a student says there are 7 paper flags. Use your model to explain the error.

Step-by-step solution

  1. For a ratio a:b, draw a sections for one quantity and b sections for the other.
  2. There are 6 equal sections.
  3. Each is 12 ÷ 6 = 2.
  4. Multiply the section counts by 2.
Answer: 6 paper; 6 fabric; the claim is 1 paper flag too many.
02 · PRACTICE

Your turn

Start with foundations, then apply the idea and explain your reasoning. Select answers below or record them on paper.

  1. Constructed response

    A mix uses 6 cups of oats for 3 cups of seeds. Complete the table (oats, seeds): (6, 3), (12, ___), (18, ___).

  2. Constructed response

    The ratio of oak trees to pine trees is 7:6. There are 52 trees altogether. Draw a tape diagram and find each count.

  3. Constructed response

    A mix uses 6 cups of oats for 6 cups of seeds. Complete the table (oats, seeds): (6, 6), (12, ___), (12, ___).

  4. Constructed response

    The ratio of oak trees to pine trees is 8:3. There are 22 trees altogether. Draw a tape diagram and find each count.

  5. Constructed response

    A mix uses 3 cups of oats for 2 cups of seeds. Complete the table (oats, seeds): (3, 2), (6, ___), (18, ___).

  6. Constructed response

    The ratio of oak trees to pine trees is 4:7. There are 44 trees altogether. Draw a tape diagram and find each count.

  7. Constructed response

    A mix uses 5 cups of oats for 2 cups of seeds. Complete the table (oats, seeds): (5, 2), (10, ___), (15, ___) and explain how you know the last two rows are equivalent.

  8. Model and error analysis

    The ratio of paper flags to fabric flags is 5:2. There are 21 flags. Part A: draw a tape diagram and enter each count. Part B: a student says there are 16 paper flags. Use your model to explain the error.

  9. Multiple select

    A recipe uses 6 cups oats for 4 cups seeds. Select all (oats, seeds) pairs that keep this ratio.

  10. Constructed response

    The ratio of oak trees to pine trees is 3:6. There are 36 trees altogether. Draw a tape diagram and find each count and the difference between the counts.

03 · CHECK

Answers and reasoning

Reveal the teacher answer key
  1. 6; 9

    Multiply both parts by 2 and by 3.

    6.RP.A.3a
  2. 28 oaks and 24 pines

    13 equal sections total; each section is 52 ÷ 13 = 4.

    6.RP.A.3a
  3. 12; 12

    Multiply both parts by 2 and by 2.

    6.RP.A.3a
  4. 16 oaks and 6 pines

    11 equal sections total; each section is 22 ÷ 11 = 2.

    6.RP.A.3a
  5. 4; 12

    Multiply both parts by 2 and by 6.

    6.RP.A.3a
  6. 16 oaks and 28 pines

    11 equal sections total; each section is 44 ÷ 11 = 4.

    6.RP.A.3a
  7. 4; 6

    Multiply both parts by 2 and by 3.

    6.RP.A.3a
  8. 15 paper; 6 fabric; the claim is 1 paper flag too many.

    There are 7 equal sections. Each is 21 ÷ 7 = 3. Multiply the section counts by 3.

    6.RP.A.3a
  9. B, D - (12, 8); (18, 12)

    Equivalent ratios multiply both quantities by the same factor. A uses 2; B uses 3. C and D change only one scaled quantity.

    6.RP.A.3a
  10. 12 oaks and 24 pines; difference 12

    9 equal sections total; each section is 36 ÷ 9 = 4.

    6.RP.A.3a
